## 4. Where the methodology is weakest, and what a referee will say

Ranked by how much they threaten publication. Each item names the file to change.

**A. The projection (D013; `research/findings/project_landscape_2026-09-06.py`).**
1. *Pipeline unit.* Programmes are proxied as one per sponsor per platform at the highest active phase. The backcast
   showed this overcounts approvable programmes by about two (30 predicted, 16 realised for 2020 to 2025 in the five
   classes), and the fix is a per-class calibration factor (CAR-T 0.45, AAV 0.50) applied by binomial thinning. A
   referee will call the calibration a fudge that absorbs whatever is wrong with the proxy. Options: (i) rebuild the
   pipeline at construct level by extending `code/01_retrieve.py` to retain intervention names (topic 01 task 3) and
   collapsing trials to constructs; (ii) restrict to sponsors with a US or EU site or an FDA or EMA regulatory history;
   (iii) use an external curated pipeline count (ASGCT quarterly landscape, Citeline) for the denominator and keep the
   registry only for phases. Whichever is chosen, re-run the backcast without calibration and report it.
2. *Transition model.* Success uses S06 point rates (F027) with an ad hoc Beta prior (n = 195 and 402), and the time
   remaining is total clinical stage times an assumed remaining share (0.70 to 1.00 at phase 1, 0.40 to 0.70 at
   phase 2, 0.15 to 0.40 at phase 3). No phase durations exist in the data. The publication-grade form is a
   multi-state model (first-in-human, phase 2, phase 3, approval, funded, treated) with transition hazards estimated
   from the cohort's own trials plus censored active programmes, or at least phase durations taken from a cited source
   (Wong, Siah and Lo 2019; BIO 2011 to 2020) with their uncertainty.
3. *Diffusion.* Patients follow S(1 - exp(-t/tau)) fitted to class-pooled penetration paths. For one-time therapies S
   is not identified (it sits at its bound); only the initial slope is. CAR-T is modelled as a class anchored on 2024
   registry totals with three assumptions (0.25 of new approvals open a new disease; Europe pool 1.5 times the US;
   Europe 0.75 of ex-US). Publication-grade: a Bass model (p, q, m) with hierarchical partial pooling across products
   (PyMC or brms-style), m tied to the label-dated pool as a prior, and leave-one-product-out predictive checks. Model
   rest of world explicitly (Japan, Australia, China registries are in `cart_by_country.csv` and `governance_china.csv`).
4. *Access probability.* The share of approvals ever funded per system is a naive proportion among products authorised
   before 2025; it ignores right-censoring of recent products and treats terminated appraisals as decisions. Replace
   with Kaplan-Meier or cumulative incidence with competing risks (funded; refused or terminated; censored) per system,
   which `research/methods/time_to_milestone.md` already specifies.
5. *Uncertainty.* The 10th to 90th bands are sampling uncertainty only. Add one-way and probabilistic sensitivity over
   the fixed assumptions (listed in `data/projection/projection_parameters.csv`), a tornado, and report structural
   alternatives (calibrated versus uncalibrated, class versus product CAR-T). Frame validation in the ISPOR-SMDM
   modelling good-practice terms: face, internal, cross and external validity; the backcast is the external validity
   test and should be shown per class.
6. *Capacity.* Centre growth is a four-point European trend applied to a single-sponsor US count (Kite, 160). The US
   governance table now has per-product centre series and the FACT count (171); use them.

**B. The revenue-to-patients conversion (`research/methods/revenue_to_patients.md`; F024, F040).** Patient counts
divide worldwide revenue by the US launch list price and apply one class factor of 0.76 calibrated on 17 disclosed
points. Referees will want product-level net prices, an explicit ex-US price assumption, and the disclosed counts used
as anchors with measurement error rather than as a check. The ASP-based variant (F040) shows the factor cannot be
replaced by US net prices alone; the missing piece is an ex-US price series (Germany's Erstattungsbetrag and France's
CEPS tariffs are already in `data/access/raw/decisions_gba.csv` and `decisions_has.csv`).

**C. Eligible pools (D010, D012; F034, F039).** Ranges are built from heterogeneous rows (gatekeeper counts, company
statements, epidemiology) with 53 documented overrides; label-dated pools borrow ranges across products and still miss
several indications (Breyanzi CLL, FL, MCL; Tecartus MCL in the US; Casgevy TDT). A referee will ask for one
consistent epidemiological method per disease (incidence times line-of-therapy shares from cited treatment-pattern
studies) with the gatekeeper counts as validation, not as sources of equal standing.

**D. Access durations (F043).** "First positive funding step" is defined per system (NICE guidance; G-BA Beschluss;
pCPA letter; JO listing or CEPS price or early access for France; AIFA determination; MSAC or PBAC support). The clock
starts at the initial EU authorisation even for extension indications in France and Canada. Fix the extension dates
from EMA procedural steps (the label events file already has them for eight products) and define the endpoint once,
with the alternatives as sensitivity.

**E. Stated-reason coding (F045).** Keyword coding of committee text in four languages, not validated. Topic 02 task 3
requires a human-coded sample with Cohen's kappa; without it the governance signatures are descriptive only.

**F. Data provenance.** Agent-retrieved rows carry URLs and statuses, but no independent audit has been done. Draw a
random sample (say 5 percent per raw table), re-open the sources, and report the error rate. Rows still
provisional: 45 in access decisions (37 of them Australian), 51 in the Singapore table, 71 in China. HAS avis rows were
upgraded to verified in the merge because they come from the official BDPM extract; say so in the paper.

**G. The companion paper.** OPEN_ITEMS items 2 and 3 (the matched non-molecular comparator; the human-validation kappa)
are still the two things every referee asked for. The maturity matrix carries a query artefact in the N-of-1 subfamily
(F038); fix it when the comparator is re-run.
